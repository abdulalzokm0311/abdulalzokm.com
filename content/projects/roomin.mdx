## Why does finding a roommate feel like rolling the dice?

Roomin is a digital platform dedicated to simplifying the often daunting
roommate search.

As UX Designer and Researcher I led a team of four designers to address trust
and transparency issues in this space. The goal was to turn roommate selection
from a risky gamble into a safer, more informed experience.

> We wanted users to feel like they were choosing a roommate, not gambling on
> one.

## Why the roommate search is broken today

**Finding roommates today is hard because** competitor platforms prioritise
listings rather than lived experience, tight move-in deadlines leave little
time to vet options, most apps have no real accountability or safety
framework, and profiles are minimal, vague and easy to fake.

**Students and young adults feel it most because** they are often moving for
the first time with little rental experience, budget constraints sharply limit
their choices, and many rely on unmoderated platforms like Kijiji or Facebook
groups.

> The result: users are expected to make a big decision with very little
> information or support. That is the gap Roomin closes.

## Listening before sketching

We ran 12 user interviews, in person and remotely, to understand concerns
about the roommate search. Participants were selected for diverse experiences.
The goal was to surface pain points and identify the features that could
meaningfully build trust and clarity.

## Walking in Dani's shoes

Dani's journey is what most users go through: cautious, curious and unsure
when reaching out. Users question whether they are really compatible, feel
skeptical during chats, and get overwhelmed by unclear communication. The
early search phase is where the trust problem lives.

## Four features, one trust problem

From anxious searching to confident choosing: show compatibility upfront,
share real experiences from past roommates, enable lifestyle based filtering,
and give users a way to flag suspicious profiles.

## What trust taught us about design

**Trust is the silent feature.** Every design decision either earns it or
spends it.

**Numbers without context are theatre.** 89% meant nothing until users could
tap it open.

**Show the differences, not just the similarities.** Trust grows when an
interface is honest about misfit rather than hiding it.

**The brief is rarely the problem.** Find a roommate was framed as housing,
but the design problem was always trust.

Next: a deeper verification process going beyond email and phone into ID and
reference checks, smarter filters driven by behaviour data, and more usability
rounds on the final design.
